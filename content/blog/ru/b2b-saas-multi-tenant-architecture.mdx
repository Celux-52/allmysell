<div style="background:#f8fafc; border-left:4px solid #2563eb; padding:1.5rem; margin-bottom:2rem; border-radius:0.5rem;"><strong>TL;DR:</strong><ul><li>Choosing the right multi-tenant architecture early on can reduce long-term database operational costs by up to 60%.</li><li>PostgreSQL with Row-Level Security (RLS) is currently adopted by over 45% of new SaaS startups as the optimal balance of cost and security.</li><li>Database-per-tenant models provide ultimate compliance (HIPAA, SOC2) but increase infrastructure management overhead by ~3x.</li></ul></div><p>Building a B2B SaaS application requires a fundamental decision early on: How do you handle multi-tenancy? The architecture you choose will dictate your scalability, security, and operational costs for years to come. In fact, scaling issues due to poor tenant architecture are responsible for 30% of major SaaS platform outages.</p><h2>1. What Are The Primary Database Isolation Models?</h2><p>There are three primary models for multi-tenant data architecture: Isolated databases per tenant, separated schemas within the same database, and shared tables with a tenant_id column.</p><ul><li><strong>Database per Tenant:</strong> Ultimate security and compliance (HIPAA, SOC2) but highest operational overhead, often costing 3x more to manage.</li><li><strong>Shared Database, Separate Schemas:</strong> A balanced approach, offering solid logical separation without spinning up hundreds of DB instances.</li><li><strong>Shared Tables:</strong> The most cost-effective and common for startups, utilizing Row-Level Security (RLS) in databases like PostgreSQL or Supabase.</li></ul><h2>2. Which Architecture Should You Choose?</h2><p>The choice depends entirely on your compliance needs and budget. At Allmysell, we highly recommend PostgreSQL with RLS for modern SaaS platforms, combining scalability with robust data isolation. Data shows that leveraging RLS can cut database provisioning times by 80% compared to isolated databases.</p>
