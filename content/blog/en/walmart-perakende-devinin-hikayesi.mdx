<div style="background:#f8fafc; border-left:4px solid #22c55e; padding:1.5rem; margin-bottom:2rem; border-radius:0.5rem;"><strong>TL;DR:</strong><ul><li>Walmart is the world's largest retailer and employer with 10,000+ stores in 24 countries and 2+ million employees.</li><li>Its "Everyday Low Price" strategy, supply chain innovation, and massive scale have transformed the global retail sector.</li><li>Walmart is now investing heavily in e-commerce, AI, and sustainability to stay competitive with Amazon.</li></ul></div><h2>Introduction</h2><p>Walmart, as one of today's largest retail chains, serves millions of customers in every corner of the world. This company, which started from a store opened in a small town in 1962, has now become a global force with more than 10,000 stores and more than 2 million employees. So, what is Walmart, how did it grow so much, and what role does it play in modern retail? In this article, we will examine Walmart's story in detail.</p><h2>What Is Walmart?</h2><p>Walmart is a large retail chain that offers a wide variety of products (food, electronics, clothing, household goods, toys, etc.) under one roof. It carries the mission of selling quality products at affordable prices by adopting the "Everyday Low Price" concept for customers.</p><p>Walmart's core strategy is to reduce costs through large-scale purchasing, efficient logistics, and minimalist store design, and to pass these savings on to customers. This model has fundamentally changed the retail sector.</p><h2>The History of Walmart</h2><h3>Founding Period (1962–1970)</h3><p><strong>1962</strong> – The first Walmart store was opened by Sam Walton in Bentonville, Arkansas. The early slogan was "We Sell for Less."</p><p><strong>1967</strong> – The first 24 stores opened and the company began rapid expansion.</p><p><strong>1970</strong> – Walmart went public and began trading on NASDAQ.</p><h3>Expansion Period (1970–1990)</h3><p><strong>1970–1980</strong> – Walmart spread rapidly across the United States. By 1980, there were over 600 stores.</p><p><strong>1988</strong> – Sam's Club (wholesale stores) opened, becoming a leader in a new market segment.</p><p><strong>1990</strong> – Walmart surpassed Sears to become America's largest retailer.</p><h3>Global Expansion (1991–2010)</h3><p><strong>1991</strong> – International operations began with entry into Mexico.</p><p><strong>2000s</strong> – Walmart operated in 15 countries including Canada, Europe, China, India, and Brazil.</p><p><strong>2006</strong> – Walmart Inc. achieved revenue exceeding $300 billion worldwide for the first time.</p><h3>Modern Era (2010–Present)</h3><p><strong>2010–2015</strong> – E-commerce and technology investments increased.</p><p><strong>2020</strong> – Online sales grew rapidly during the COVID-19 pandemic. Walmart was recognized as an "essential business."</p><p><strong>2023</strong> – Walmart remained the world's largest employer and retailer. Annual revenue exceeded $600 billion.</p><p><strong>Today</strong> – Walmart is a global leader serving 2+ billion customers in 24 countries.</p><h2>Key Features of Walmart</h2><h3>1. Low Price Strategy</h3><p>Walmart offers the industry's lowest prices through supply chain optimization and high-volume sales. This creates customer loyalty.</p><h3>2. Diverse Product Range</h3><p>Everything from food to electronics, clothing to household goods is sold in one store. This "one-stop shopping" experience makes Walmart the preferred shopping destination.</p><h3>3. Strong Logistics Network</h3><p>Walmart has its own distribution centers and delivery systems. This enables fast inventory management and satisfactory delivery.</p><h3>4. Technology Investments</h3><p>Investments in artificial intelligence, data analytics, and automation make Walmart a pioneer in modern retail.</p><h3>5. Social Responsibility</h3><p>Walmart takes important steps in employee rights, environmental protection, and supporting local communities.</p><h2>Walmart's Business Model</h2><h3>All-In-One Design</h3><p>Walmart stores are designed for customers to find everything. This convenience factor is an important advantage against competitors.</p><h3>Supply Chain Management</h3><p>Walmart works directly with manufacturers, eliminating intermediaries. This reduces costs and controls prices.</p><h3>Data Analytics</h3><p>Walmart optimizes product selection by analyzing customer behavior. As a result, inventory waste is minimized in every store.</p><h2>Advantages of Walmart</h2><ul><li><strong>✅ Affordable Prices</strong> – The industry's most competitive pricing ensures customer appeal.</li><li><strong>✅ Wide Product Selection</strong> – Almost everything can be found in a single store.</li><li><strong>✅ Easy Accessibility</strong> – Customers can find a Walmart nearby in thousands of locations.</li><li><strong>✅ Reliability</strong> – Consistent service and product quality make Walmart the preferred brand.</li><li><strong>✅ Employment</strong> – Walmart provides millions of job opportunities in developed and developing countries.</li><li><strong>✅ E-Commerce Integration</strong> – Integration of online and physical stores offers an "omnichannel" experience.</li></ul><h2>Challenges of Walmart</h2><ul><li><strong>⚠️ Employee Rights Debates</strong> – Low wages and working conditions have been criticized.</li><li><strong>⚠️ Impact on Local Businesses</strong> – Walmart opening typically causes small businesses to close.</li><li><strong>⚠️ Environmental Concerns</strong> – Large-scale operations create environmental impact.</li><li><strong>⚠️ E-Commerce Competition</strong> – Amazon has surpassed Walmart in online sales.</li><li><strong>⚠️ Management Challenges</strong> – Managing such a large chain is complex and difficult.</li></ul><h2>Walmart's Impact on the Retail Sector</h2><p>Walmart has transformed the retail sector in the following ways:</p><h3>1. Price Competition</h3><p>Walmart's low-price model has created a standard that forces the entire sector into price competition.</p><h3>2. Technology Application</h3><p>Walmart's pioneering in computer systems, barcodes, and data management has been adopted across the industry.</p><h3>3. Supply Chain Innovation</h3><p>Walmart's logistics network has become a model for the industry.</p><h2>Walmart's Future</h2><h3>E-Commerce Integration</h3><p>Walmart is making massive investments in online sales to compete with Amazon.</p><h3>AI and Automation</h3><p>Store automation, warehouse robots, and artificial intelligence applications are being increased.</p><h3>Sustainability</h3><p>Walmart is taking steps toward the goal of becoming carbon neutral.</p><h2>Conclusion</h2><p>Walmart has transformed from a small-town store into one of the world's largest retail players. Sam Walton's "Everyday Low Price" vision has simplified the lives of millions of customers. With evolving markets and technological advancement, Walmart's impact on the retail sector will continue. However, it will need to cope with challenges such as e-commerce, employee rights, and environmental issues.</p><p>Walmart is not just a shopping destination but a corporate model invested in modern retail. Its story witnesses the transition from small business to large corporation in the United States and its integration into the global economy.</p><h3>References</h3><ul style="font-size: 0.9rem; color: #64748b;"><li>Walmart Inc.
